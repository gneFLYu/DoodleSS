## Assistant Final Response

```text
Let
\[
R_i=\operatorname{res}^{Q_8}_{C_4\langle i\rangle},
\]
and write \(\sigma\) for the sign representation of \(C_4\) and \(\lambda\) for its two-dimensional rotation representation. The first thing to restrict is the **grading**:

\[
\sigma_i|_{\langle i\rangle}=1,
\qquad
\sigma_j|_{\langle i\rangle}=\sigma_k|_{\langle i\rangle}=\sigma,
\qquad
\mathbb H|_{\langle i\rangle}=2\lambda.
\]

Consequently, the useful restriction dictionary is:

| \(Q_8\)-class | Image at \(C_4\langle i\rangle\) | Precision |
|---|---|---|
| \(u_{\sigma_i}\) | \(1\) | In the mod-2 Thom/2-BSS convention, after choosing the Thom generator. There is no integral \(Q_8\)-orientation class \(u_{\sigma_i}\) by itself. |
| \(u_{\sigma_j}\), \(u_{\sigma_k}\) | \(u_\sigma\) | Mod-2/formal Thom notation, up to an \(\mathbb F_4^\times\)-unit. Since \(\sigma\) is not integrally orientable, \(u_\sigma\) is not an ordinary integral orientation generator. |
| \(u_{2\sigma_i}\) | \(1\) | Honest even-dimensional orientation normalization. |
| \(u_{2\sigma_j}\), \(u_{2\sigma_k}\) | \(u_{2\sigma}\) | Honest oriented class. |
| \(D\) | \(\varepsilon_D\Delta_1\), \(\varepsilon_D\in W(\mathbb F_4)^\times\) | \(\Delta_1=\bar{\mathfrak d}_1^{,2}u_{2\lambda}u_{2\sigma}\). One may normalize \(D\) so that \(\varepsilon_D=1\), but that is a choice. |
| \(h_1\) | \(\eta=\bar s_1a_{\sigma_2}\) | Exact with DKLLW/BBHS names. |
| \(h_2\) | \(\bar{\mathfrak d}_1u_\lambda a_\sigma\) | Included because it is commonly used with restriction arguments. |

The associated Euler classes give a useful normalization-free check:

\[
R_i(a_{\sigma_i})=0,
\qquad
R_i(a_{\sigma_j})=R_i(a_{\sigma_k})=a_\sigma.
\]

Indeed, \(\sigma_i\) becomes trivial on \(\langle i\rangle\), whereas \(\sigma_j\) and \(\sigma_k\) become the sign representation.

The symbols \(x\) and \(y\) require more care. They are individual generators on the **mod-2 2-BSS input**, not canonical surviving generators of the integral \(Q_8\)-HFPSS \(E_2\)-page. They satisfy Bockstein differentials such as
\[
d_1(x)=2y^2,\qquad d_1(y)=2x^2.
\]
Therefore a table asserting canonical integral formulas for \(R_i(x)\) and \(R_i(y)\) is misleading: their individual images depend on the chosen chain-level/eigenvector basis and units. What is source-independent is the restriction of surviving composites. For example,

\[
R_i(c)=R_i(Dxh_1)=0,
\]
while
\[
R_i(d)=R_i(D^2x^2)
=\bar{\mathfrak d}_1^{,4}u_{4\lambda}u_{2\sigma}a_{2\sigma}\ne0.
\]

Thus \(R_i(xh_1)=0\), because \(R_i(D)\) is invertible, while \(R_i(x^2)\) lies—up to a Witt unit—on the \(C_4\)-line generated by \(u_{2\sigma}^{-1}a_{2\sigma}\). The same warning applies to \(y^2\): compare exact composites, not unsupported aliases for \(x\) and \(y\) separately.

Finally, \(R_i\) is multiplicative and commutes with every differential:
\[
R_i(d_r z)=d_r(R_i z).
\]
This is why the \(C_4\)-HFPSS can force or bound \(Q_8\)-differentials. Changing the subgroup changes the dictionary: for instance, \(\sigma_i|_{\langle j\rangle}=\sigma\), so \(u_{2\sigma_i}\) restricts to \(u_{2\sigma}\), not to \(1\). This subgroup dependence explains several apparently contradictory restriction formulas in the working notes.

These formulas are DKLLW Proposition A.9 together with the representation/Thom conventions and the unit warning used in Sections 2 and 4. :codex-file-citation{path="E:\课程\PACE2025_fly\HFPSS Q_8\[Formal DKLLW] s42543-024-00087-7.pdf" purpose="source"}
```
